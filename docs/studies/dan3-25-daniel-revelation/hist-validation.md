## Layer 2: Grounding and Classification Check

### Issue 1: I4 Day-Year Classification Could Be Stronger Per HIST DB Evidence

**Section:** Inferences Table, I4
**Nature:** The study classifies the day-year principle as I-A(1) but the "Why this is an inference" column states "The specific starting and ending dates (538, 1798) require historical identification of events not stated in the text." This is fair. However, the study's description of the day-year principle's grounding is thin compared to what the HIST DB provides.

The study cites "Num 14:34 and Ezek 4:6 establish the day-for-year pattern. The sealing command (Dan 12:4) and scope coherence require extended time. The yamim qualifier (Dan 10:2-3 vs. 9:24) signals non-literal time." The HIST DB provides 13+ arguments for the day-year principle including (1) the 70-weeks empirical fulfillment (457 BC + 483 = AD 27, verified by Luke 3:1-2 synchronism), (2) miniature symbolization, (3) scope argument, (4) pre-Adventist tradition, (5) Daniel's physical collapse being disproportionate to 6.3 literal years. The study mentions some of these but could note that the day-year principle has been reclassified in the methodology (the study references "methodology day-year reclassification section" but the grounding column could be clearer).

**Assessment:** This is a minor grounding completeness issue, not a misclassification. The I-A(1) HIGH classification is correct. The study accurately acknowledges the historical date endpoints as inferential while recognizing the textual basis for the principle itself. The HIST position is not weakened by this presentation.

**Recommendation:** No change strictly required. If the update agent wants to strengthen: add a parenthetical in the I4 "What the Bible actually says" column noting that the 70-weeks empirical fulfillment (457 BC + 483 = AD 27) provides independent validation of the day-year principle. This is already implicit in the methodology reference but could be more explicit.
